## 1. Executive Summary

Curis is a connected healthcare operating system designed to bring the full care journey together: patient intake, appointments, triage, consultation, prescriptions, diagnostics, pharmacy dispensing, health records, inventory, billing, payment validation, patient access, and operational oversight.

The product is functionally complex, but the brand must never feel complicated, cold, bureaucratic, or dominated by control language. The identity should make Curis feel like a living system built around people rather than a rigid system imposed on them.

The selected **Human Orbit** direction achieves that by placing a warm living core at the centre of an open, energetic orbital form. The central core represents the person receiving care. The surrounding forms represent clinicians, staff, records, departments, and services moving together around one continuous care journey. The identity is optimistic, fluid, modern, nurturing, and recognizably human without relying on a generic medical cross, heart, shield, or hospital symbol.

The strategic idea is simple:

> **Healthcare has many moving parts. Curis keeps them moving around people.**

This brand system therefore balances two truths:

1. Curis is a serious operational platform used for sensitive clinical and financial activity.
2. Healthcare is fundamentally human, emotional, relational, and full of life.

The visual and verbal identity should lead with the second truth and support it with the first. Curis should feel warm before it feels technical, clear before it feels complex, and alive before it feels institutional.

### Recommended master tagline

> **Care moves with you.**

### Recommended functional descriptor

> **The connected healthcare operating system.**

### Recommended brand promise

> **Curis keeps every care journey connected, clear, and centred on people.**

### Strategic confidence

- Fit with the selected Human Orbit direction: **92%**
- Fit with the need for a warm, natural, vibrant identity: **90%**
- Ability to remain credible with healthcare decision-makers: **85%**, provided the visual system is disciplined
- Risk of being mistaken for a wellness-only brand: **25%** with the guidelines in this document; approximately **55%** without them
- Risk of becoming visually generic: **20%** when the custom mark, wordmark, motion language, and colour discipline are properly implemented

## 2. Source-of-Truth Hierarchy

The brand must remain aligned with three sources:

1. **Curis Platform Project Scope** — the authority for product function, audience, boundaries, claims, workflows, and product terminology.
2. **Human Orbit concept board** — the authority for the selected emotional and visual direction.
3. **This brand identity system** — the authority for naming, positioning, messaging, visual usage, tone, and cross-channel consistency.

When a marketing idea conflicts with the product scope, the product scope prevails. Curis must never be presented as a payment processor, autonomous diagnosis tool, insurance adjudicator, public medical marketplace, or generic appointment application.

# Part I — Brand Foundation

## 3. Brand Name and Naming Architecture

### 3.1 Primary brand name

# **Curis**

The market-facing product name is **Curis**.

Curis is short, distinctive, easy to display in a digital interface, and broad enough to support the platform as it expands across clinical, administrative, patient, and operational experiences. The name suggests care, continuity, coordination, and thoughtful progress without making an explicit promise to cure a medical condition.

### 3.2 Legal differentiator

**Curis by Citrus** is the legal differentiator and ownership signature. It should not replace the everyday product name.

Use **Curis by Citrus** in:

- Contracts and commercial agreements
- Legal pages and regulatory notices
- Trademark and ownership statements
- First-use introductions in formal presentations
- Press releases where ownership must be clear
- Footer statements
- Formal invoices issued by Citrus Labs Limited
- App-store publisher references

Use **Curis** in:

- Website navigation
- Product UI
- Dashboard headers
- Social media
- Customer support language
- Marketing campaigns
- Merchant onboarding
- Patient communications
- Everyday speech

### 3.3 Ownership line

Recommended legal ownership line:

> **Curis is a product of Citrus Labs Limited.**

Recommended compact signature:

> **Curis by Citrus**

### 3.4 Capitalization

- Correct: **Curis**
- Correct legal signature: **Curis by Citrus**
- Incorrect in running text: CURIS
- Incorrect: Curis By Citrus
- Incorrect: Curis by citrus
- The custom logo may use a lowercase visual wordmark, but written references should use title case: **Curis**.

### 3.5 Module and portal naming

Curis should operate as one masterbrand. Departments and portals are functional expressions of Curis, not independent subbrands.

Recommended interface naming:

- Curis for Front Office
- Curis for Triage
- Curis for Physicians
- Curis Pharmacy
- Curis Laboratory
- Curis Health Records
- Curis Finance
- Curis Patient
- Curis Administration

These labels should not receive separate logos or unrelated colour systems. They should inherit the Human Orbit identity and use restrained functional accents where useful.

### 3.6 Naming guardrails

Do not create separate consumer-facing brands for every department. That would fragment recognition and contradict the purpose of one connected operating system.

Avoid names that make Curis sound like:

- A hospital chain
- A health insurer
- A telemedicine-only application
- A digital wallet
- A diagnostic laboratory
- A wellness or lifestyle application
- An autonomous clinical decision-maker

### 3.7 Trademark recommendation

Before public launch, complete jurisdiction-specific trademark clearance for:

- The word **Curis**
- The **Human Orbit** symbol
- The combined Curis wordmark and symbol
- The primary tagline where registration is commercially useful

No visual identity document can substitute for a formal legal clearance process.

## 12. Tone of Voice

### 12.1 Core voice principles

Curis speaks with:

- **Warm clarity**
- **Calm confidence**
- **Active, direct language**
- **Human respect**
- **Optimistic energy**
- **Practical specificity**

### 12.2 Writing style

Use:

- Short, clear sentences
- Familiar words
- Active voice
- Positive instruction
- Descriptive labels
- Reassuring next steps
- Specific outcomes
- “You” and “your” when appropriate
- “People,” “patients,” and “care teams,” not abstract “users” in public-facing copy

Avoid:

- Corporate jargon
- Technical language without explanation
- Overuse of “governance,” “compliance,” “ecosystem,” and “digital transformation” in public messaging
- Fear-based language
- Medical overpromises
- Cute language in high-risk situations
- Unnecessary exclamation marks
- Robotic success messages
- Blaming the person for an error

### 12.3 Voice calibration by context

#### Brand and marketing

Warm, inspiring, energetic, visual.

Example:

> **Care has many moving parts. Curis brings them together around people.**

#### Product onboarding

Friendly, encouraging, concise.

Example:

> **Let’s set up your branch so your team can start moving care forward.**

#### Clinical workflow

Calm, precise, neutral.

Example:

> **Complete the required consultation details before finalising this record.**

#### Patient communication

Warm, respectful, reassuring.

Example:

> **Your health report is ready. You can view or download it securely from your Curis account.**

#### Finance and billing

Clear and factual, never cold.

Example:

> **This payment is waiting for facility confirmation. We’ll let you know as soon as it has been reviewed.**

#### Urgent or sensitive events

Direct, serious, and action-oriented. Remove playful language and decorative celebration.

Example:

> **Urgent review required. Open the patient record now and follow your facility’s emergency procedure.**

### 12.4 Microcopy examples

| Situation | Avoid | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Save success | Record saved successfully | Your changes are saved. |
| Report ready | Report generation completed | Your health report is ready. |
| Empty appointments | No data found | No appointments here yet. New appointments will appear as soon as they are scheduled. |
| Failed save | Error 500 | We could not save this yet. Your information is still here—check your connection and try again. |
| Payment pending | Pending finance validation | Your payment details are being reviewed by the facility. |
| Access denied | Unauthorized | You do not have access to this section in your current role. |
| Session expiry | Token expired | Your secure session has ended. Sign in again to continue. |
| Form validation | Invalid input | Check the highlighted fields and add the missing information. |

### 12.5 Language safety

Do not imply that Curis:

- Diagnoses independently
- Guarantees medical outcomes
- Replaces clinicians
- Holds or processes patient funds
- Makes insurance decisions
- Eliminates all medical or operational risk

Prefer “supports,” “helps,” “connects,” “coordinates,” “makes clearer,” and “keeps moving.”

# Part IV — Storytelling and Messaging

## 13. Core Brand Story

Healthcare rarely happens in one place, one moment, or one screen. A person may move from reception to triage, consultation, diagnostics, pharmacy, records, billing, and follow-up. Every step matters, but the experience can easily become fragmented.

Curis was created to help those moving parts work as one connected journey.

At the centre is a person. Around them are care teams, departments, decisions, records, and services. Curis helps each part move in relation to the others, so the journey feels clearer for staff and more continuous for patients.

Curis does not replace the human work of care. It helps that work stay connected.

> **Care moves. Curis helps it move together.**

---

## 14. Human Orbit Story

The Human Orbit symbol is not a decorative letter C. It is a visual model of the Curis experience.

### The living core

The warm central sphere represents the person at the centre of care. It may also represent the shared care context that every authorised role contributes to.

### The orbiting forms

The surrounding ribbons represent the many people, departments, and touchpoints involved in a healthcare journey. They move independently but remain connected to a shared centre.

### The human gesture

The warm upper form and circular element suggest a person in motion, a caring interaction, or one person reaching toward another. The symbol remains abstract enough to avoid becoming a literal parent-and-child, clinician, or patient icon.

### The open form

The orbit is open rather than closed. It suggests movement, access, continuity, growth, and the next step in a journey.

### The colour relationship

Cool colours represent clarity, intelligence, and connected systems. Warm colours represent people, care, empathy, and vitality. Their interaction expresses Curis’s defining balance: technology with a human centre.

# Part V — Visual Identity System

## 18. Logo Concept: Human Orbit

### 18.1 Strategic concept

The Human Orbit mark represents a connected healthcare system that moves around a living human centre.

It combines:

- A subtle open **C** for Curis
- A central living core
- Fluid orbital pathways
- A human gesture
- Forward movement
- A balance of cool intelligence and warm care

### 18.2 What the logo should communicate

- Human-centred care
- Movement and continuity
- Warmth and empathy
- Modern connected technology
- Optimism
- Collaboration
- A complete but open journey

### 18.3 What the logo should not communicate

- Security software
- A hospital chain
- A wellness spa
- A children-only service
- An insurance company
- A generic medical cross
- A cryptocurrency or financial product
- A gaming application

### 18.4 Final-production refinement

The selected concept should be redrawn as true vector geometry rather than directly tracing a raster concept image.

Refinement priorities:

1. Reduce excessive gloss and artificial highlights.
2. Preserve soft dimensionality without making the mark look like a 3D emoji.
3. Simplify overlapping ribbons so the silhouette remains clear at small sizes.
4. Make the central core slightly less dominant than in the generated concept.
5. Establish consistent terminal shapes across all orbital forms.
6. Create a deliberately simplified favicon, not merely a scaled-down master mark.
7. Develop a custom Curis wordmark with recognisable letter details.
8. Ensure the mark works in flat colour and monochrome.

## 20. Logo Usage Specifications

### 20.1 Clear space

Use the diameter of the central core as the base unit **X**.

Minimum clear space around the full lockup:

- Left and right: **0.75X**
- Top and bottom: **0.5X**

For the standalone mark, use **0.5X** on all sides.

### 20.2 Minimum sizes

Recommended minimum digital sizes after vector refinement:

- Full horizontal lockup with “by Citrus”: **140 px wide**
- Compact lockup: **100 px wide**
- Standalone mark: **24 px**
- Simplified favicon: **16 px**

Recommended minimum print sizes:

- Full lockup: **35 mm wide**
- Standalone mark: **8 mm**

### 20.3 Background rules

Preferred backgrounds:

- Nurture Cream
- Cloud White
- Orbit Navy
- Very light warm neutral
- Approved calm photography with sufficient clear space

Avoid:

- Busy clinical photographs behind the logo
- Similar-value blue or coral backgrounds
- Multi-colour gradients competing with the mark
- Placing the full-colour logo over charts or dense UI

### 20.4 Logo misuse

Do not:

- Replace colours with an arbitrary rainbow
- Stretch, rotate, skew, or outline the mark
- Add a medical cross, shield, heart, or stethoscope
- Rearrange the orbital forms
- Change the central core into a literal face
- Add shadows not defined in the master artwork
- Use the full-dimensional mark at tiny sizes
- Place the tagline inside the logo lockup by default
- Create department-specific versions of the logo
- Use a low-resolution raster file where SVG is supported

## 21. Favicon and App Icon

The favicon must be a purpose-built micro-mark.

### 21.1 Simplified construction

At 16–32 px:

- Use the strongest outer orbital C form.
- Keep one warm inner core.
- Remove minor internal ribbons and small decorative details.
- Use a dark navy or cream background tile where required.
- Preserve a minimum one-pixel separation between visible forms.

### 21.2 App icon

Recommended app-icon treatment:

- Rounded-square container
- Orbit Navy or Nurture Cream background
- Simplified Human Orbit mark centred optically
- No wordmark
- No “by Citrus”
- No tagline
- Controlled gradient with strong small-size contrast

### 21.3 Required favicon package

- `favicon.ico` containing 16×16, 32×32, and 48×48
- `favicon-16x16.png`

- 15% Cloud White
- 8% Curis Blue or Living Cyan
- 7% warm accents

- Primary action: Curis Blue

## 27. Motion Identity

Motion is essential to Human Orbit because the concept is based on continuous care.

### 27.1 Motion principles

- Fluid, not mechanical
- Responsive, not frantic
- Gentle acceleration and deceleration
- Purposeful, not decorative
- Human-scale timing

### 27.2 Recommended timings

- Button and hover response: 120–180 ms
- Card transitions: 180–240 ms
- Page-level transitions: 240–360 ms
- Brand hero motion: 600–1,200 ms

### 27.3 Signature motion

The orbital forms may enter independently and settle around the central core. The core should remain visually stable while the surrounding elements move.

This reinforces the idea that the person remains at the centre while the system coordinates around them.

### 27.4 Product motion

Use motion to:

- Confirm a completed step
- Show progression
- Maintain context between screens
- Explain movement between care stages
- Indicate that content is loading

Do not use celebratory motion for:

- Diagnosis
- Urgent clinical alerts
- Payment rejection
- Access denial
- Clinical record corrections
- Sensitive health-report delivery

### 27.5 Reduced motion

All motion must respect system-level reduced-motion settings. Provide non-animated equivalents.

# Part VI — Product Experience

## 28. UI Design Principles

### 28.1 Human first, task clear

Every page should make the person, task, or next step immediately understandable.

### 28.2 Warmth around the work

Operational interfaces should remain clean and calm. Warmth should come through colour accents, microcopy, illustration, spacing, and moments of acknowledgement—not through decorative clutter.

### 28.3 Brand at the edges, function at the centre

Use the full Human Orbit expression in login, onboarding, empty states, launch screens, and major transitions. Use a restrained system inside complex clinical and financial workflows.

### 28.4 One clear next action

Primary actions should be visually obvious. Avoid multiple equally loud buttons.

### 28.5 Preserve context

Patients, visits, branches, roles, and workflow states should remain visible where relevant so users do not lose orientation.

### 28.6 Calm density

Curis contains complex data. Use grouping, spacing, hierarchy, progressive disclosure, and purposeful colour instead of making everything large or decorative.

## 44. Brand Risks and Mitigations

### Risk 1: The identity becomes too wellness-oriented

**Cause:** excessive florals, soft pink, lifestyle photography, and vague care language.

**Mitigation:** show real clinical and operational contexts; retain Orbit Navy and Curis Blue; use the functional descriptor.

### Risk 2: The identity becomes childish

**Cause:** excessive rounded shapes, bright colours, playful illustration, and casual microcopy.

**Mitigation:** maintain disciplined typography, clear hierarchy, credible product screenshots, and context-aware tone.

### Risk 3: The identity becomes generic health-tech

**Cause:** replacing Human Orbit with a simple C, cross, heart, or dots.

**Mitigation:** protect the custom Human Orbit geometry, motion language, and human-centred narrative.

### Risk 4: The platform loses B2B credibility

**Cause:** showing only patient lifestyle imagery and avoiding operational proof.

**Mitigation:** pair warm storytelling with real workflows, role experiences, implementation evidence, and measurable business value.

### Risk 5: Vibrancy becomes visual chaos

**Cause:** using every colour in every component.

**Mitigation:** use a neutral base, one cool accent, and one warm accent in most layouts.

### Risk 6: Fun undermines serious moments

**Cause:** applying delight indiscriminately.

**Mitigation:** follow the emotional calibration model in this document.
